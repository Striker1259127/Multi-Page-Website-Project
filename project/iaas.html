IAAS : Infrastructure as a Service

What is IAAS?

IAAS is a cloud computing model where a vendor provides on-demand access to fundamental computing resources over the internet. This includes virtual or physical servers, storage, networking, and data center space. Out of the three, it is also the most open type of service by far, as the user gets to choose items such as the OS, compute your own resources, storage options, networking, applications, and security services. The main issue tha comes with IAAS systems however is the fact that you are the one that will be responsible for managing that all systems are up to date when it comes to all the thing I've previously mentioned.

Item providers

With IAAS, there are a variety of providers avaliable that make the use of such systems. These include...

  • Amazon Web Services
  • Google Cloud Platform
  • Microsoft Azure
  • IBM Cloud
  • Oracle Cloud Infrastructure

Pros and Cons

Pros

Efficient Costs and Easy Scalability
With IAAS systems, you only pay for the resources that you need or have physically used, making it a very strong in cost efficiency. This ties in strongly with scalability as IAAS systems are very easy to grow without spending more on newer hardware or spending more altogether in order to reach a cartain checkpoint where you are allowed access to necesary resources like in SAAS systems.
Low server maintinence
With these systems, tThe cloud provider manages physical hardware maintenance, data center facility management, heating/cooling, and core infrastructure repairs. To put it into simple terms, the large majority when it comes to server hardware and handling is controlled by the vendor. In case of a disaster, there is always a redundancy placed in the systems so that if one server goes down, the information needed for your service to work will still be operational.
Speed
Finally there is the speed factor. With IAAS, creating virtual servers or network environments takes minutes rather than weeks or months required for physical server procurement. Instead of facing the hassle of setting up physical servers that require literal miles of wire connection and design, all you have to do is set up the site and BAM, you have saved yourself thousands of hours worth of setup and millions of dollars in server investment.

Cons

Excessive Control
Although IAAS is known for its creative liberty when it comes to allowing the user to design and apply their own ingenuity, the main issue comes with the sheer amount of responsibility that you get with such a system. Keep in mind that you are entirely responsible for securing the operating system, applications, data, and access controls. All the provider will give is the access to your itemology to be saved in their server and some basic security measures. This leads to a lot of poeple feeling frustrated as such a system can more often than not be very time intensive. Hence, most servers that use IAAS has a large spectrum of individuals working together to keep it operational.
Cloud dependance and outage issues
Due to such a dependance on these systems, if the cloud vendor experiences a major outages or network disruption, your services are affected without your direct control over the physical fixes. As an example we can take the AWS crash that happened in March of this year. Due to an object hitting a data-center in the UAE, they forfully had to shut the servers down, causing a two day outage, affecting hundreds of servers in the process.
Vendor Lock-Ins
Iaas working on its own with one server type is great. All the workflows and data is easily streamlined into one continous chain. Bring in another system that you may try to integrate and that is where the problems arise. With IAAS, m,igrating complex cloud infrastructure and workloads from one IaaS provider to another can be technically difficult and expensive. This can be a hassle to deal with and especially troublesome for larger companies, so integrating these servers is not usually something that is often done.

What is PAAS?

PAAS is a cloud computing service model where a third-party provider delivers hardware and software tools over the internet—typically those needed for application development. Compared to the SAAS model, Paas is much more lenient when it comes to security and app management, as you have the power to choose on what security measures as well as what apps you get to choose to apply to your systems. Unlike IAAS however, Paas is still a bit restrictive in other sections such as what OS, runtime tools, and infrastructure is needed. Most of this is handled by the provider, often making this type of service model very likable by many

PAAS is special in its own way as many people have grown accustomed to, and built their own services that arte loved by all. Some incude...

  • Amazon Web Services : Elastic Beanstalk
  • Microsoft Azure App Services
  • Salesforce App Cloud
  • IBM Cloud Foundary
  • Red Hat Openshift

Pros and Cons

Pros

Faster Devlopment Time
Since PAAS systems allows the use of self run application and moderation of said services, developers don't have to worry abut long configuration of eviorments.
Reduced costs
Replaces monthly subscription with one that acts as a "Pay as you go" type of subscription. It also eliminates the use of a in-house management team for upholding infrastructure.
Creative freedom
PAAS, unlike SAAS, is much more open when it comes to security. The consumer can decide to choose their own way of securing their own computer alongside some built-in safety protocols.

Cons

Vendor Lock-Ins
When working with PAAS systems, a strong platform reliance on frameworks and database integrations makes migration to another provider complex and costly.
Limitation on software
Like I have said before, PAAS doesn't allow much access to the underlying hardware, operating system configurations, or custom server runtimes, limiting deep customization options that many businesses may need.
Integration challenges and price risks
When working alone, PAAS systems work amazingly. Integrating that system to on-site databases however may lead to integration and latency issues. This goes hand in hand with price hikes. while pricing does scale with usage with the quoted "Pay as you go" strategy, sudden spikes in application traffic or unoptimized application logic (Like Integration latency) can lead to unexpectedly high monthly bills.
